#### Text 1

```
Homework has never been particularly popular with students and even many parents, but in recent years has been especially controversial. School districts across the country, most recently Los Angeles Unified, are rewriting their thinking on this educational ritual. Unfortunately, L.A. Unified has produced an inflexible policy which mandates that with the exception of some advanced courses, homework may no longer count for more than 10% of a student's academic grade.
```

```
This rule is meant to address the difficulty that students from impoverished or chaotic homes might have in completing their homework. But the policy is unclear and confused. Certainly, some homework is useful, but the purpose of homework should be to enhance learning, not to undermine it. If the district is essentially, giving a pass to students who do not do their homework, while expecting the same performance from those who do, it is sending a very bad message.
```

```
District administrators say that homework will still be a part of schooling; grades, as long as they are less than 10%, will count toward a student's academic grade. But many teachers say that, given the limited time available for instruction, they are reluctant to spend time in class correcting homework. Students who did incomplete homework the night before are unlikely to do so tomorrow night, either. What about the implications for teachers who assigned the homework? The homework ban might actually be a good idea, but the possibility that teachers could be penalized for assigning homework is troubling.
```

```
At the same time, the policy addresses none of the truly thorny questions about homework. For instance, how much homework should students be assigned? There is a good deal of evidence that homework is useful for students, but the amount of homework should be moderate. Research suggests that excessive homework can lead to stress and burnout. Moreover, the policy does not address the issue of quality. A teacher who assigns meaningful, well-designed homework is doing far more good than one who assigns mindless worksheets.
```

```
What the policy does not mention is that homework should be guided by the student's ability level. One-size-fits-all assignments do not serve the interests of all students. Some students need more practice; others need less. A more thoughtful approach would allow teachers to tailor homework to the needs of their students.
```

```
Finally, the policy needs to be evaluated within the school board, which is responsible for setting educational policy. The board needs to consider the implications of its actions and conduct public hearings. It is not too late for L.A. Unified to do homework right.
```
